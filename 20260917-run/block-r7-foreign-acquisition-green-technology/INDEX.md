## Details kept out of the figures

**01 — acquisition registry.** An acquisition here is a reported foreign share crossing 10% (the first 10%+ answer after an earlier answer below 10%). It uses the same rule and dates as R2-08, and it is not a verified purchase. All 2020 ownership answers are treated as unknown, because 47% of firms report 10%+ in 2020 against about 9% in other years. As filed, 854 firms would start in 2020; with the repair, 56 get a later year and 798 count as never foreign-owned. Starts in 2017 (no 2016 file) and 2021 (2020 unknown) are dated approximately. There are 844 acquirers: 372 up to 2015 and 472 after. The 8,011 firms never seen 10%+ foreign-owned are the comparison firms. Left out: 1,213 firms that are foreign-owned when first seen and 395 with no usable answer. Shares are almost all 0% or 100%, so the 50% and above-0% rules pick nearly the same firms (772 and 850 starters; CSV). Only 112 of the 559 acquirers with a later answer are still 10%+ at that answer, and 447 are back below. In this file that comes from the fictional firm links. There are two design choices. First, the "back below 10%" counts are light-gray bars next to each year's acquisitions, because dots inside the dark bars were unreadable. Second, 2020 is a dashed box with the as-filed count written in, because an 854 bar would shrink every other bar to about one eighth. Firm identity across mergers cannot be checked here (fictional links).

**02 — acquirers seen per year since acquisition, before vs after Paris.** A firm counts in a year when its energy use (GJ), CO2 and value added are all above zero. Energy records in doubt (importers with unknown export status in 2017 and 2019–2022, as in R2-08) are left out. A year since acquisition gets its own point in later R7 charts only when at least 30 acquirers from before Paris AND 30 from after have a usable year in it. The widest symmetric window passing this rule is −4 to +4; years further out are grouped at the two ends. The window is computed in every file, never typed. It fails at +5 (17 after-Paris acquirers) and −6 (no before-Paris acquirer). Firms acquired up to 2015 are seen mostly after their acquisition (369 in the year before, 365 in the acquisition year, 115 at +4). Firms acquired later are seen mostly before it (228, 294, 40). 367 (before Paris) and 315 (after) acquirers have usable years both before and from the acquisition inside the window. The 8,011 firms never seen 10%+ foreign-owned are the comparison firms; 3,397 of them have 2+ usable years. Leaving out records in doubt removes 38% of after-Paris acquirers' usable years in the window but only 6% of before-Paris ones. Within each period the rule removes more years from the acquisition onward than before it: 10% vs 0% for acquisitions up to 2015, 44% vs 33% for later ones. It also removes acquirers' years more often than comparison firms' years, because acquirers import more. With these records kept the window would be −5 to +5. Other versions (CSV only): exactly dated acquisitions only give a window of −3 to +3; 2020 ownership answers as filed, −4 to +4; majority (50%+) acquisitions, −4 to +4. All later R7 files keep the main window. The file defines the shared "R7 SAMPLE AND WINDOW" block that R7-03 to R7-07 copy unchanged. Firm links are fictional, so these counts test the method only.

**03 — who gets a foreign owner.** Each dot is one regression: the acquirer's row in its last year below 10% foreign (the year just before for 681 of 844; for 2017 and 2021 acquirers an earlier year) against every row of the 8,011 firms never seen 10%+ foreign-owned, within year × 2-digit industry cells, standard errors clustered by firm. Before the acquisition, acquirers have 129% more workers, 313% more value added and 376% more energy used, but about the same energy per unit of value added (+2.8%, interval −9.6% to +16.8%) and CO2 per unit of value added (+6.8%, −6.1% to +21.4%): far bigger, not clearly cleaner. Their energy per unit of value added was not changing differently in the year before (+1.0%, 313 acquirers). They export (+16.5 points) and import (+15.7) more; in the acquisition year 50% of those not exporting start exporting (8% of comparison firms; +41.0 points) and 47% start importing (18%; +25.0) — so later before/after figures mix a new owner with new trade. Checks (CSV and regression table): exactly dated acquirers only, adding year × province cells, and majority (50%+) only give the same reading; acquirers up to 2015 and after 2015 are both much larger than comparison firms, with energy per unit of value added −4.8% and +12.5%, neither clear. Keeping the energy records in doubt turns the change rows to −18.6% / −18.2% (clear): that comes from 2017 records in doubt before 2018 reference years (62 of 85 acquirers) and 2019 records in doubt (44 of 67), i.e. the recording jump and fall, not firms — the reason the main version leaves them out. Starts importing without 2017 (import recording jump): +27.4 points. The 2022 export question covers direct exports only (no firm answers "no"), so 2022 export answers are not used; the export answer is known for 403 acquirers. Nominal values only; firm links are fictional, so the start rows and the "already changing" rows are a test of the method here.

**04 — energy and CO2 per unit of value added around a foreign acquisition.** Event study: log energy (GJ) or CO2 per unit of value added on one marker per year since the acquisition (−4 to +4, the window set in R7-02; ends grouped), firm and year effects, standard errors clustered by firm; comparison = firms never seen 10%+ foreign-owned (0 on every marker); 760 acquirers and 3,397 comparison firms with 2+ usable years (19,756 firm-years); energy records in doubt left out. Against the year just before the acquisition, nothing clear happens afterwards: energy per unit of value added averages −3.4% over years 0 to +4 (interval −18.0% to +13.7%), CO2 per unit of value added −2.8% (−17.3% to +14.2%). But every year before sits above 0 (+11% to +25% for energy; jointly not clear, p = 0.41): against the earlier years before (−4 to −2), years 0 to +4 are −18.7% for energy (−32.3% to −2.5%, clear) and −15.2% for CO2 (−29.0% to +1.2%, not clear). Much of that fall comes before the ownership change, so it cannot be credited to the new owner. Checks in the regression table and CSV: Sun–Abraham (the 81 acquirers of 2017 left out, no row the year before) −0.3%; every acquisition and record as filed −10.6% (energy) and −12.9% (CO2), but its years before are far below 0 (−57% at ≤−5 and −3; p < 0.001), which is why the 2020 ownership repair matters; energy records in doubt kept −16.0% (−28.7% to −1.1%) and −15.1%. Those records sit more often after the acquisition than before it (20% of acquirers' usable years before the acquisition, 27% from it on; up to 2015 0% vs 10%, later 33% vs 44%), and they record a fifth to a seventh of other rows' energy per unit of value added. With a separate year effect for them (review check), the doubt-kept average is −8.6% (−21.7% to +6.7%, not clear). All coefficients of the main, as-filed and Sun–Abraham versions equal minus R2-08's foreign-owner estimates (same window). We do not compare these numbers with the ~30% in Bu Deasy's note. Energy and CO2 per unit of value added are not technology, and CO2 differs from energy only through the fuel mix. Nominal values; firm links are fictional, so this tests the method only.

**05 — size, energy used and energy per unit of value added after a foreign acquisition.**
- **Model.** R7-04's event study, main version, run once for each of 12 measures: one marker per year since the acquisition (−4 to +4, ends grouped), firm and year effects, standard errors clustered by firm. The comparison firms are those never seen 10%+ foreign-owned. 760 acquirers and 3,397 comparison firms, 19,756 firm-years. Fuels burned uses 17,473 firm-years and bought electricity 18,723: firm-years with none are left out (2,497 and 1,243 of 23,577 main firm-years). Energy records in doubt are left out.
- **Two averages per measure.** Black = years 0 to +4 against the year before (as R7-04). Gray = the same years against years −4 to −2.
- **Against the year before, nothing is clear.** Energy used −4.9% (interval −18.6% to +11.2%), value added −1.5% (−12.8% to +11.3%), energy per unit of value added −3.4% (−18.0% to +13.7%).
- **Against the earlier years before.** Energy per unit of value added is −18.7% (−32.3% to −2.5%, clear). That is mostly because value added is +19.8% (+4.3% to +37.5%) while energy used is −2.7% (not clear). In logs this is exact: −0.207 = −0.027 − 0.180. Output (+17.1%) and workers (+10.3%) move with value added.
- **The growth came first.** Value added was already rising before the acquisition (years before: p = 0.001; year by year −0.32, −0.17, −0.10, 0 log points from −4 to −1, then flat). So the lower energy per unit of value added is not a sign that foreign owners made firms greener.
- **Energy used does not rise.** Unlike the scale expansion in Bu Deasy's note, energy used does not rise after the acquisition in this file.
- **Fuel mix changes little.** CO2 per GJ +0.6% (black) and +4.3% (gray, clear). Bought electricity share +0.4 and +3.8 points (gray, clear). Bought electricity GJ −16.1% against the year before (just clear; −4.4% gray), which reads as weak: one clear result among 24 averages.
- **Checks.** In logs, energy (CO2) per unit of value added = energy used (CO2) minus value added, and the same holds for output and CO2 per GJ. This is checked marker by marker to 1e-6. The energy and CO2 per unit of value added rows equal R7-04's main version, coefficient by coefficient. The as-filed, doubt-kept and Sun–Abraham versions are in R7-04, and robustness is in R7-07. Year-by-year coefficients of all 12 measures are in the CSV.
- **Limits.** We do not compare these numbers with the ~30% in Bu Deasy's note. The file has no equipment or process data. CO2 = energy used × standard factors. Values are nominal. Firm links are fictional, so this tests the method only.
