## Details kept out of the figures

**01 — the groups, fixed before COVID.**
- **Cohort.** The cohort and its later states come from the block "COVID COHORT AND STATES", copied unchanged from R4-01 (`block-r4-covid-survival/01_cohort_states_flow.do`, Version 2). Of the 3,007 firms in the 2019 file, 2,266 were also in the 2017 or 2018 file. They are R10's main sample. The 741 firms first seen in 2019 appear only in the row "Main rule + firms new in 2019".
- **Groups.** They are formed once from the 2017, 2018 and 2019 answers, and later answers never change them. The unusable 2020 foreign-ownership answers are never read. Main rule: in the group if yes in at least half of the known answers, otherwise "other firms". A firm with no known answer is left out, never counted as "no".
- **Main-rule counts.** Exporters 402 / other 1,202 / unknown 662 (29%). Importers 1,329 / 937. Foreign-owned 127 / 2,139.
- **"Other" firms that said yes at least once.** 59 other exporters, 377 other importers and 297 other foreign-owned firms said yes in one of the three files (CSV column `n_other_some_yes`). The stricter rule (always yes vs never yes) leaves them out and is a named check in R10-06. Its foreign-owned group has only 22 firms, too few for estimates.
- **Other rules.** "2017–2018 answers only" (Bu Deasy's literal "pre-2019") gives 346 exporters and 283 foreign-owned firms. "2019 answer only" gives 227 exporters, but the 2019 export answer is unknown for 1,159 firms (51%).
- **Overlap** (table only, not drawn). 65% of exporters and 79% of foreign-owned firms also import. From 2017 the files record far more importers than before (likely a recording change), so 59% of these firms count as importers.
- **Support table** (`_support.csv`). For each group and file 2017–2022 it counts the firms usable for each outcome. There is no productivity residual in 2017. In 2022 the export answer is known only from the direct-export question (1,013 firms; `export_flag` is known for 125, all yes). The smallest main group-file cell is 63 (foreign-owned, 2022).
- **Real files needed.** Firm links are fictional in the development data, so which 2017–18 answers belong to a 2019 firm, and every count of firms leaving the files, means nothing here.

**02 — firms still in the survey files after COVID, by group.**
- **Meaning of "still in".** A firm counts as still in the files in a year if it is in that year's file or a later one, so a firm that misses one survey and comes back counts as still there. The files have no closure record: leaving the files is not proof of closing. The last file cannot be rescued by a later one, which is true for both starting files, so the comparison stays fair.
- **Firms and groups.** 2,266 firms of the 2019 file that were also in 2017 or 2018, grouped once from their 2017–2019 answers (R10-01 rule, blocks copied unchanged). Normal-years comparison: 1,796 firms of the 2012 file, grouped from 2010–2012 and followed 2013–2015. 2012 is the only possible normal-years start (two earlier files and three consecutive later files before 2020; no 2016 file).
- **Still in the 2022 file (group vs other firms).** Exporters 60.4% vs 67.1%; importers 63.8% vs 67.9%; foreign-owned 49.6% vs 66.4%. In the 2015 file (2012 firms): 57.8% vs 71.2%; 57.7% vs 69.6%; 49.1% vs 68.8%.
- **Gap to other firms of the same industry and province** (percentage points, average of the three later files, 95% range). COVID years: exporters 0.0 (−4.8 to 4.8), importers 0.0 (−3.9 to 3.9), foreign-owned −9.4 (−17.1 to −1.7). Normal years: −4.2 (−9.3 to 0.9), −3.2 (−8.2 to 1.8), −7.6 (−15.6 to 0.3). Year-by-year gaps: gaps CSV and regression table only.
- **COVID minus normal** (Bu Deasy's × Post2020 for survival; one model, errors clustered by firm, 867 firms in both starting files): exporters +4.2 (−2.8 to 11.2), importers +3.2 (−3.1 to 9.5), foreign-owned −1.8 (−12.8 to 9.2). All three ranges cross zero: on this file, internationalised firms did not leave the files more (or less) in the COVID years than in normal years. The one clear gap (foreign-owned firms leave more often) is there in normal years too.
- **Lines vs gaps.** The lines compare all firms. The gaps hold industry and province fixed, which shrinks them (exporters, 2019 firms: −5.2 raw, 0.0 adjusted). On the development file the fictional links are drawn within province and industry, so this is expected.
- **Importers.** Import answers are recorded differently from 2017: importers are 59% of the 2019 firms but 17% of the 2012 firms, so panel (b) compares two different kinds of group.
- **Real files needed.** Firm links are fictional in the development data, so who stays in the files or leaves them means nothing here. The file runs unchanged on the real BPS panel, where "still in" is the survival measure.
