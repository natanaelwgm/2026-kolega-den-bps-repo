## Details kept out of the figures

**01 — the groups, fixed before COVID.**
- **Cohort.** The cohort and its later states come from the block "COVID COHORT AND STATES", copied unchanged from R4-01 (`block-r4-covid-survival/01_cohort_states_flow.do`, Version 2). Of the 3,007 firms in the 2019 file, 2,266 were also in the 2017 or 2018 file. They are R10's main sample. The 741 firms first seen in 2019 appear only in the row "Main rule + firms new in 2019".
- **Groups.** They are formed once from the 2017, 2018 and 2019 answers, and later answers never change them. The unusable 2020 foreign-ownership answers are never read. Main rule: in the group if yes in at least half of the known answers, otherwise "other firms". A firm with no known answer is left out, never counted as "no".
- **Main-rule counts.** Exporters 402 / other 1,202 / unknown 662 (29%). Importers 1,329 / 937. Foreign-owned 127 / 2,139.
- **"Other" firms that said yes at least once.** 59 other exporters, 377 other importers and 297 other foreign-owned firms said yes in one of the three files (CSV column `n_other_some_yes`). The stricter rule (always yes vs never yes) leaves them out and is a named check in R10-06. Its foreign-owned group has only 22 firms, too few for estimates.
- **Other rules.** "2017–2018 answers only" (Bu Deasy's literal "pre-2019") gives 346 exporters and 283 foreign-owned firms. "2019 answer only" gives 227 exporters, but the 2019 export answer is unknown for 1,159 firms (51%).
- **Overlap** (table only, not drawn). 65% of exporters and 79% of foreign-owned firms also import. From 2017 the files record far more importers than before (likely a recording change), so 59% of these firms count as importers.
- **Support table** (`_support.csv`). For each group and file 2017–2022 it counts the firms usable for each outcome. There is no productivity residual in 2017. In 2022 the export answer is known only from the direct-export question (1,013 firms; `export_flag` is known for 125, all yes). The smallest main group-file cell is 63 (foreign-owned, 2022).
- **Real files needed.** Firm links are fictional in the development data, so which 2017–18 answers belong to a 2019 firm, and every count of firms leaving the files, means nothing here.

**02 — firms still in the survey files after COVID, by group.**
- **Meaning of "still in".** A firm counts as still in the files in a year if it is in that year's file or a later one, so a firm that misses one survey and comes back counts as still there. The files have no closure record: leaving the files is not proof of closing. The last file cannot be rescued by a later one, which is true for both starting files, so the comparison stays fair.
- **Firms and groups.** 2,266 firms of the 2019 file that were also in 2017 or 2018, grouped once from their 2017–2019 answers (R10-01 rule, blocks copied unchanged). Normal-years comparison: 1,796 firms of the 2012 file, grouped from 2010–2012 and followed 2013–2015. 2012 is the only possible normal-years start (two earlier files and three consecutive later files before 2020; no 2016 file).
- **Still in the 2022 file (group vs other firms).** Exporters 60.4% vs 67.1%; importers 63.8% vs 67.9%; foreign-owned 49.6% vs 66.4%. In the 2015 file (2012 firms): 57.8% vs 71.2%; 57.7% vs 69.6%; 49.1% vs 68.8%.
- **Gap to other firms of the same industry and province** (percentage points, average of the three later files, 95% range). COVID years: exporters 0.0 (−4.8 to 4.8), importers 0.0 (−3.9 to 3.9), foreign-owned −9.4 (−17.1 to −1.7). Normal years: −4.2 (−9.3 to 0.9), −3.2 (−8.2 to 1.8), −7.6 (−15.6 to 0.3). Year-by-year gaps: gaps CSV and regression table only.
- **COVID minus normal** (Bu Deasy's × Post2020 for survival; one model, errors clustered by firm, 867 firms in both starting files): exporters +4.2 (−2.8 to 11.2), importers +3.2 (−3.1 to 9.5), foreign-owned −1.8 (−12.8 to 9.2). All three ranges cross zero: on this file, internationalised firms did not leave the files more (or less) in the COVID years than in normal years. The one clear gap (foreign-owned firms leave more often) is there in normal years too.
- **Lines vs gaps.** The lines compare all firms. The gaps hold industry and province fixed, which shrinks them (exporters, 2019 firms: −5.2 raw, 0.0 adjusted). On the development file the fictional links are drawn within province and industry, so this is expected.
- **Importers.** Import answers are recorded differently from 2017: importers are 59% of the 2019 firms but 17% of the 2012 firms, so panel (b) compares two different kinds of group.
- **Real files needed.** Firm links are fictional in the development data, so who stays in the files or leaves them means nothing here. The file runs unchanged on the real BPS panel, where "still in" is the survival measure.

**03 — output, workers and productivity around COVID, firms still in the files.**
- **Firms and groups.** 2,266 firms of the 2019 file also in 2017 or 2018, grouped once from their 2017–2019 answers (R10-01 rule, blocks copied unchanged). Stayers = the 1,484 in every file 2020–2022: 243 of 402 exporters, 848 of 1,329 importers, 63 of 127 foreign-owned firms. Results for stayers are conditional on staying. R10-04 counts leavers as closed (largest possible loss), so 03 is the smallest-selection view and the truth lies between.
- **Measures.** Output = value of goods produced. Workers = persons employed. Productivity = value added per worker (value added = output minus materials, energy and services bought). All at current prices: the file has no deflators, and the industry × year averages absorb any industry-wide price change. Table-only checks (`_checks.pdf`): gross output per worker, the productivity residual (none in 2017), and firm + year averages only.
- **Model.** Log outcome on firm averages, industry × year and province × year averages, and group × each file (2019 = 0); errors clustered by firm. Two numbers are printed per panel (stayers). "2020–22 vs 2019" is the average of the three COVID-year markers. "2020–22 vs 2017–19" is Bu Deasy's × Post2020 term: its own regression on the same rows.
- **Exporters were already moving before COVID.** Relative to similar firms, exporters' output was 21% (−33 to −7) lower in 2017 and 12% lower in 2018 than in 2019, and their value added per worker 26% and 18% lower (all but output 2018 clear; before-COVID p 0.022 and 0.005). The × Post2020 term is therefore +17% (+3 to +33) for output, but against 2019 alone the COVID years are only +4% (−8 to +19), not clear. About three quarters of the +17% is the rise before COVID. It must not be read as "exporters grew faster because of COVID". Value added per worker: +12% (−2 to +28) against 2017–19, −5% (−18 to +11) against 2019. Gross output per worker (checks table) shows the same pattern. Exporters' workers: +8% (−1 to +19) against 2019, +2% (−7 to +12) against 2017–19.
- **Importers.** Value added per worker fell well behind similar firms: 2020 −14%, 2021 −34%, 2022 −37% (all clear); −29% (−38 to −19) against 2019 and −31% (−39 to −23) against 2017–19. The before-COVID markers are not clear (p 0.477). Workers rose: +9% (+2 to +17) and +11% (+3 to +18). Output: −12% (−22 to −2) against 2019, −10% (−19 to +1) against 2017–19.
- **Foreign-owned (10%+).** Nothing clear (63 stayers): output +7% / +13%, workers −2% / +5%, value added per worker +4% / +5%.
- **All firms (hollow) vs stayers.** Close everywhere on development data. The "already apart" flag follows the stayers, the firms behind the printed numbers. Among all firms two more panels have a clear 2017 marker: importers' output −16% (−24 to −7) and foreign-owned value added per worker −29% (−47 to −5).
- **Limits.** Only two files before COVID (no 2016 file), so an unclear before-COVID marker is weak evidence of equal paths. Association, not the effect of COVID. Firm links are fictional in the development data, so who stayed means nothing here: the file runs unchanged on the real BPS panel.

**04 — output and workers, with a what-if that counts leavers as closed.**
- **Measure.** Each firm's change since 2019 = 200 × (level − 2019 level) ÷ (level + 2019 level). 0 = no change, −200 = fell to zero, and small changes read like ordinary % changes. The scale can hold firms that leave, which a log cannot. Output is gross output in current prices (the file has no price index). Value added per worker is not shown, because a closed firm has none.
- **Firms.** The 2,266 firms of the 2019 file also in 2017 or 2018, grouped once from their 2017–19 answers (R10-01 rule; blocks copied unchanged from `01_covid_cohort_groups.do`). Dots: the 1,484 firms in every file 2020–2022. They are the same firms, counts and models as R10-03, but R10-03 uses logs, so its numbers differ slightly (exporters' output against 2017–19: +17% there, +13 here).
- **The what-if (squares).** The 782 firms missing from every later file are counted as closed from the first file they miss: 395 gone from 2020, 202 from 2021, 185 from 2022. That makes 1,774 closed firm-years, of which 1,760 are in the models. The files record no closures. For each firm this is the largest possible loss, but not for the gap between groups: a group that loses more firms is pulled down more. **The true gap need not lie between dots and squares.** Where the two differ, this exhibit cannot tell.
- **Printed per panel, % points (in every file / what-if).** First "2020–22 vs 2019", then "2020–22 vs 2017–19" (Bu Deasy's × Post2020).
  - Exporters, output: +4 (−7 to +14) / +4 (−7 to +15), then +13 (+2 to +23) / +12 (+1 to +24).
  - Exporters, workers: +8 (+1 to +16) / +8 (−3 to +18), then +3 (−5 to +11) / +3 (−8 to +13).
  - Importers, output: −11 (−19 to −2) / −8 (−17 to +1), then −8 (−17 to +1) / −2 (−12 to +7).
  - Importers, workers: +8 (+2 to +14) / +6 (−2 to +14), then +10 (+4 to +16) / +7 (−1 to +16).
  - Foreign-owned, output: +6 (−10 to +23) / −14 (−31 to +3), then +10 (−7 to +28) / −9 (−27 to +9).
  - Foreign-owned, workers: −2 (−15 to +11) / −18 (−34 to −2), then +4 (−9 to +18) / −16 (−33 to +1).
- **Reading.**
  - No panel is clear in both versions against 2019.
  - Exporters' output is clear in both versions only against 2017–19, because exporters were already 17 and 10 points lower in 2017 and 2018: the change came before COVID.
  - "Already apart before COVID" is printed for exporters' output and workers and importers' output. It is flagged from either version; for importers' output only the 2017 square is clear.
  - Foreign-owned firms flip sign between the versions (they leave the files more often, R10-02), so there is no conclusion for them.
- **Totals** (table only). Each group's total as % of its 2019 total in 2022, leavers at 0 (the lowest possible) / in every file: all firms' output 67.6 / 124.2; exporters 89.3 / 146.3; foreign-owned 28.2 / 83.3. Masked values make totals jumpy, for example other importers' output in every file reaches 284.4.
- **Real files needed.** Firm links are fictional in the development data, so who left the files, and every square, means nothing here. The file runs unchanged on the real BPS panel.

**05 — share of firms exporting around COVID, with normal years.**
- **Firms and groups.** The 2,266 firms of the 2019 file also in 2017 or 2018, grouped once from their 2017–19 answers (R10-01 rule; blocks copied unchanged from `01_covid_cohort_groups.do`). Normal years: the 1,796 firms of the 2012 file, grouped from 2010–12 and followed to 2015. Each line is the % exporting among the firms that answered the export question in that file (an unknown answer is left out, never counted as "no"). A point is drawn only if at least 30 firms answered.
- **Printed numbers.** The model is exporting (100 or 0) on firm averages, industry × year and province × year averages, and group × each file; errors are clustered by firm. The numbers are in percentage points against firms of the same industry and province. "2020–21 vs 2019" is the average of the 2020 and 2021 markers. "2020–21 vs 2017–19" is Bu Deasy's × Post2020 term. The normal-years number uses the same two later files (2013–14 vs 2012). COVID minus normal comes from one model with both start files.
  - Exporters: −42 (−51 to −32) against 2019, −42 (−49 to −35) against 2017–19; normal years −36 (−46 to −25); COVID minus normal −6 (−20 to +8).
  - Importers: −5 (−12 to +3), −3 (−9 to +3); normal −1 (−12 to +9); difference −3 (−16 to +9).
  - Foreign-owned (10%+): −22 (−39 to −6), −21 (−35 to −7); normal −3 (−23 to +17); difference −19 (−45 to +6).
- **Exporters' fall is mostly fall-back, not COVID.** Exporters were chosen for exporting in 2017–19, so their share was high then by construction: 78.7% in 2019, then 42.8% in 2020 and 46.3% in 2021. Firms picked the same way in 2012 fell from 66.2% to 36.3% one file later, with no COVID. COVID minus normal years (−6) is not clearly away from 0.
- **Foreign-owned firms** fell more than in normal years, but with 77–81 group firms the difference (−19) is not clear.
- **Importers** hardly differ from other firms in either period. Import answers are recorded differently from 2017: importers are 59% of the 2019 firms but 17% of the 2012 firms, so gray and black importers are different kinds of group.
- **Before COVID.** No 2017 or 2018 marker is clearly away from 0 (before-start p 0.35, 0.26, 0.76). With only two earlier files this is weak evidence that the paths were the same. In the normal-years model, 2011 is clearly above 2012 for exporters (+18) and importers (+19); the printed normal-years number is against 2012 alone, so it is not affected.
- **2022.** The 2022 file asks about direct exports only (no firm answers "no" to the general question), so 2022 is drawn dotted from the direct-export answer and kept out of every printed number. With 2022 included (regression column 3), exporters are −46 against 2019 and −46 against 2017–19.
- **Table-only versions.** Firms in every file 2020–22: exporters −38 against 2019. Firm and year averages only: −37.
- **Real files needed.** Firm links are fictional in the development data, so following a firm across files means nothing here. The file runs unchanged on the real BPS panel.
