# R9 — Who decarbonised: within/reallocation/entry/exit accounting

**Planning only. This row is incomplete; no analysis was executed. All44 plans must pass the global freeze before execution.**

## Question

Did manufacturing become cleaner because factories improved, or because production moved toward cleaner factories?

Explain aggregate environmental intensity with exact accounting using the single implementation owned by D4.

Unit: Verified plant at two calendar endpoints and accounting interval×group×measure×component; frame evidence distinguishes observed entry/exit from business birth/death.

### Target and meaning

A_t = sum_i s_it a_it = sum_i C_it / sum_i V_it, s_it=V_it/sum_i V_it, a_it=C_it/V_it; Delta A = W+B+X+EN+EX

The weighted plant intensity equals the aggregate ratio on the exact same positive-denominator universe. FHK within, between, cross, entry and exit terms sum to the observed change; MP provides a separate exact decomposition.

This is accounting, not a causal regression. Frame movement, reporting attrition and industry/trade reclassification can mimic economic entry or exit. A negative intensity change is improvement under this orientation.

### Formula symbols

- **i**: a verified establishment, never a row-number or artificial linkage.
- **t**: actual calendar year.
- **V**: value added deflated with a certified appropriate price index; any single-output-deflator approximation is named.
- **Y**: gross output deflated with its certified output-price index.
- **E**: scope-defined verified physical energy in GJ; incomplete observed subtotals are separately named.
- **C**: scope-defined verified CO2 in metric tonnes; not automatically all greenhouse gases.
- **L**: workers under an annually verified employment definition.
- **X**: predetermined measured covariates declared for the particular design.
- **g**: verified treatment-entry cohort in calendar years.
- **k**: calendar event time t-g.
- **S**: a verified trade, ownership, industry, or survival state; unknown is retained as unknown.
